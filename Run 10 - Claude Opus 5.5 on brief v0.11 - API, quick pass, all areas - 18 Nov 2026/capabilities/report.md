# Capability forecast

This forecast was made on 2026-11-18, in a separate piece of work. It forecasts the score on the Epoch Capabilities Index that the most capable AI system would get, released or not, and separately the highest score held by a released system. Epoch Capabilities Index, Bayesian version adopted by Epoch AI about 4 November 2026. The scale is fixed by two reference models: Claude 3.5 Sonnet (released June 2024) = 130 and GPT-5 (released August 2025) = 150. All scores here are as published in Epoch's data file on 18 November 2026 [capabilities-001, capabilities-002]. A 20-point gap is therefore roughly the gap between a good mid-2024 model and the best model of August 2025.

## Today

- **Most capable released system:** GPT-6.2 Astra (statistically tied with Claude Fable 5.2 at 173.50) (OpenAI), score 174.2, released 2026-11-17.
- **Most capable unreleased system:** about 3 months ahead of the released frontier (80% range 1 to 7 months).
- **How the highest score has moved:** Record released scores on today's scale: GPT-4 121.6 (Mar 2023); Claude 3.5 Sonnet 130 (Jun 2024); o1 141.2 (Dec 2024); o3 147.1 (Apr 2025); GPT-5 150 (Aug 2025); Gemini 3 Pro 153.0 (Nov 2025); GPT-5.5 Pro 162.8 (Apr 2026); GPT-6 Astra 169.2 (Sep 2026); Claude Fable 5.2 173.5 (9 Nov 2026); GPT-6.2 Astra 174.2 (17 Nov 2026). The record rose 17.8 points in the year to November 2025 and 21.1 in the year to November 2026. Epoch's fitted trend is 8.0 points a year before reasoning models and 16.6 since; Epoch says the two newest scores, which are provisional, sit above that trend [capabilities-001, capabilities-002, capabilities-003].
- **Other measures:** Task length: METR found the best public model in Feb-Mar 2026 completed software tasks that take a skilled person about 12 hours half the time (range 5-61 hours), but only about 1.5 hours at 80% success; its task suite cannot measure much beyond 16 hours [capabilities-005, capabilities-036]. The doubling time of this length has shortened to about 89 days since 2024 [capabilities-011]; extrapolating, today's best systems manage roughly one to two working weeks at 50% (my estimate, not a measurement) [capabilities-045]. Many hard benchmarks are saturated [capabilities-036]; GPT-6.2 Astra produced machine-checked solutions to 7 of 68 maths problems unsolved as of August 2026 [capabilities-004]. At Anthropic, AI wrote over 80% of approved code by May 2026 (developer claim) [capabilities-012], but a controlled trial of late-2025 tools found only 4-20% speed-ups [capabilities-038]. These measures cover mainly well-specified, checkable tasks and say little about messy, long, real-world work.
- **What is known about unreleased systems:** METR, given the best internal models of four developers in Feb-Mar 2026, found the internal frontier on average about 2 months ahead of the public one [capabilities-005]. In November METR reported three Anthropic internal-only models finished training July-October 2026, the latest scoring above 50% on all Anthropic's internal benchmarks, and that Anthropic now holds models slightly longer before release [capabilities-009]. OpenAI cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra's release over deceptive behaviour and is reusing its base model [capabilities-023], and reports an internal model producing results on open maths problems (secondary reports) [capabilities-026]. Claude Mythos tiers are partner-only versions of the released Fable models [capabilities-025]. Withheld does not always mean ahead: Google's delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro reportedly trailed [capabilities-028]. Two leading releases came 1 and 9 days ago, so the gap is probably near its low point. Estimate: 3 months, about 5 points [capabilities-040].

## The forecast

Score of the most capable AI system, released or not, by percentile:

| Date | 1 | 5 | 10 | 25 | 50 | 75 | 90 | 95 | 99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the end of 2028 | 190 | 199 | 204 | 215 | 228 | 243 | 261 | 277 | 320 |
| the end of 2031 | 203 | 218 | 230 | 256 | 302 | 360 | 450 | 530 | 760 |

Highest score held by a released system (10th, 50th and 90th percentiles): the end of 2028 200, 221, 248; the end of 2031 226, 288, 405.
Released and unreleased systems: Today the gap is about 3 months, roughly 5 points. I expect it to widen to about 4 months (about 8 points) by end-2028 and 5-8 months (about 15 points at the median) by end-2031, more in faster worlds and less in slower ones. Developers are already holding models longer [capabilities-009], cancelling releases on safety findings [capabilities-023] and crossing their own self-improvement risk thresholds [capabilities-024]; the more AI speeds up research, the more a model is worth as an internal tool. Competition between developers pushes the other way. A binding pause or mandatory review would make the two converge. Released scores above about 200 also depend on Epoch adding harder benchmarks, which could bias published scores downward.

- Chance the score rises faster per year between today and the end of 2031 than the released record's pace over the past two years (19.4 points a year): 63%.
- Chance it rises less than half as fast: 10%.
- Scores beyond what the benchmarks can measure: Above roughly 200 my numbers are translations, not predicted benchmark results. I project the rate of progress (points per year) implied by the inputs and by AI's help with research, as if harder benchmarks existed, keeping the scale's existing meaning. For task length I use about 6 points per doubling of the 50% time horizon up to week-scale tasks, as in the past record [capabilities-002, capabilities-011], rising to 10-15 points per doubling beyond month-scale tasks, because longer projects need many dependent steps and real-world feedback that benchmarks do not test [capabilities-041]. Above about 400 the number is a convention meaning 'progress far faster than any past pace', not a measured amount of capability. Thresholds for the two probabilities start from today's most capable system, about 179 (174.17 plus a 5-point internal lead): faster than past pace means above 278.5 at end-2031 (my 37th percentile); less than half means below 228.8 (my 10th percentile) [capabilities-044].

## The five bands at the end of 2031

### Band 1, least capable: 5% of the probability (below the 5th percentile; scores below 218)

- **What AI systems can do:** Typical score about 209. Clearly more useful than today's systems but not transformative. Agents reliably carry out well-specified software, data and analysis jobs lasting a few weeks, with human review, and give strong help in mathematics and science, occasionally solving open problems. They still fail unpredictably on long, messy or vaguely specified work, need people to check important outputs, and cannot run research programmes or organisations on their own.
- **Length of task done on their own:** Well-defined tasks: about 4-8 months of skilled human work at 50% success, about 2-4 weeks at 80%. Open-ended tasks (vague goals, real-world feedback): about 2-4 weeks at 50%, a few days at 80%. Converted with the rule in beyond_benchmarks and uncertain by a factor of several.
- **Research on AI:** AI writes most code and runs routine experiments, about half the labour of AI research; people choose ideas and judge results. Progress in AI is about 1.2-1.5 times what it would be with the same compute and no AI help, because compute, or a pause, is the binding limit.
- **Compared with people:** Better than most professionals at bounded, checkable tasks and at expert level in coding and competition-style mathematics. Below the best experts at multi-month projects, choosing research problems, management and judgement under ambiguity. No advantage in physical work.
- **Released systems:** About 2-4 months, a few points. With slow progress, holding models back is worth less; a pause or mandatory review would make released and internal systems converge.
- **How it comes about:** Several setbacks combine after 2026: gains from reinforcement learning flatten as Ord and You warned [capabilities-015, capabilities-016]; a financial correction cuts data-centre spending in 2028 [capabilities-020, capabilities-022]; a serious incident or court action brings binding limits or a multi-year pause on frontier training [capabilities-023]; the measured AI research speed-up proves to be benchmark overfitting [capabilities-008]. The pace falls to about 8-12 points a year during 2027 and 3-6 from 2028.

### Band 2: 20% of the probability (5th to 25th percentile; scores 218 to 256)

- **What AI systems can do:** Typical score about 239. Agents do most well-specified software engineering end to end, including multi-week projects with light review, and much routine scientific analysis. They routinely produce publishable mathematics and solve a large share of open-problem benchmarks. They are weaker at long ambiguous projects, new strategy and coordinating real-world work; people set goals and sign off on key decisions.
- **Length of task done on their own:** Well-defined: about 1-3 years of skilled human work at 50% success, 2-4 months at 80%. Open-ended: about 2-3 months at 50%, about 2 weeks at 80%.
- **Research on AI:** AI does about 70-80% of research engineering and runs most experiments; people pick directions and interpret results. Progress in AI is about 1.5-2 times what it would be with the same compute and no AI help, limited by compute for experiments.
- **Compared with people:** Better than nearly all professionals at bounded intellectual tasks; at top-expert level in coding and mathematics. Comparable to good researchers at carrying out a given plan; below the best at choosing problems and long-range judgement.
- **Released systems:** About 3-6 months, 4-8 points: longer safety review and staged releases, as with Mythos and GPT-6.1 Astra [capabilities-025, capabilities-023].
- **How it comes about:** Roughly the recent pace (17-20 points a year) holds through 2028 on the 2027 spending surge [capabilities-021], then slows to about 7 a year in 2029-31 as debt, power and memory limits curb compute growth [capabilities-020, capabilities-018, capabilities-019], reinforcement-learning returns shrink [capabilities-015], and AI labour runs into limited experiment compute [capabilities-006, capabilities-033].

### Band 3, middle: 50% of the probability (25th to 75th percentile; scores 256 to 360)

- **What AI systems can do:** Typical score about 302. AI does most knowledge work that can be specified and checked. It builds and maintains large software systems, runs research programmes in mathematics, computer science and computational biology under modest human direction, and produces original results routinely. Limits are slow real-world feedback (laboratory experiments, markets, regulation), physical work, poorly defined goals, and how far people can trust and oversee it.
- **Length of task done on their own:** Well-defined: projects that would take one skilled person a decade or more (a team several years) at 50% success; about a person-year at 80%. Open-ended: about 6-12 months at 50%, 1-2 months at 80%. These lie beyond what current benchmarks can measure.
- **Research on AI:** AI does about 90% of AI research work, including proposing many of the ideas; people set the agenda, approve large experiments and oversee safety. Progress in AI is about 2-3 times what it would be with the same compute and no AI help; compute for experiments is the main bottleneck.
- **Compared with people:** Better than the best people at most well-defined intellectual tasks; comparable to top researchers across much of research. Still behind the best people in some open-ended judgement, in tasks with long real-world feedback loops, and in physical work.
- **Released systems:** About 4-8 months, 10-20 points: the best systems are kept internally longer for research and testing, and released versions are restricted in cyber and biology uses.
- **How it comes about:** Spending above $1 trillion in 2027 [capabilities-021] and fast revenue growth [capabilities-034] keep compute growing through 2028-29. The AI research speed-up measured in 2026 [capabilities-007] holds near its public-data level (about 1.3-1.5 times) and grows slowly, giving about 22-25 points a year throughout despite slower compute growth after 2029. No binding pause.

### Band 4: 20% of the probability (75th to 95th percentile; scores 360 to 530)

- **What AI systems can do:** Typical score about 420. Outperforms the best people at almost all intellectual work done at a computer: science where experiments are cheap or simulated, engineering design, strategic analysis, software of any size. Many copies working in parallel compress years of a field's progress into months. Limits are physical experiments, manufacturing, energy, and institutions' ability to absorb and check results.
- **Length of task done on their own:** Time is no longer a good measure. Well-defined: at 50% success, projects the size of a large team's multi-year effort; at 80%, multi-person-year projects. Open-ended: about 1-2 years at 50%, several months at 80%. These are extrapolations.
- **Research on AI:** AI does over 95% of AI research; people oversee, set constraints and approve. Progress in AI is about 4-8 times what it would be with the same compute and no AI help; AI labour output is far larger, but compute and deliberate safety slowdowns limit the gain.
- **Compared with people:** Superhuman at nearly all cognitive tasks, including research taste in most fields. People keep advantages in physical work, legitimacy, accountability and deciding what should be done.
- **Released systems:** About 6-18 months, tens of points: the frontier is held by developers, and possibly governments, as a research tool; released versions are deliberately limited.
- **How it comes about:** The roughly two-fold acceleration METR measured at Anthropic [capabilities-007] is confirmed across developers and compounds; the share of research done autonomously passes half in 2027-28 [capabilities-012]; returns to research effort stay above 1 [capabilities-013, capabilities-031, capabilities-032]. A system that outperforms top AI researchers arrives about 2028-29, and the pace reaches 40-80 points a year in 2029-31.

### Band 5, most capable: 5% of the probability (above the 95th percentile; scores above 530)

- **What AI systems can do:** Typical score about 650 (a formal extrapolation). A full software intelligence explosion. Systems are far beyond the best people on every intellectual task that can be tested and generate major scientific and engineering advances faster than people can check them. Remaining limits are compute, energy, physical experiments and the speed of building things.
- **Length of task done on their own:** Not a meaningful measure for either kind of task: anything that can be done at a computer gets done, limited only by the time real-world interaction takes.
- **Research on AI:** Essentially all of it; people cannot follow the details. Progress in AI is ten or more times what it would be with the same compute and no AI help.
- **Compared with people:** Far beyond the best people in almost all intellectual domains; human oversight depends on AI-assisted checking.
- **Released systems:** Very large or undefined: the most capable systems are probably never released, and the released record stops tracking the frontier.
- **How it comes about:** AI research is fully automated by 2027-28; returns to research are well above 1, and experiment compute does not bind because better-chosen experiments substitute for it [capabilities-013, capabilities-033]; one developer's compute runs the equivalent of millions of top researchers [capabilities-014]; there is no effective pause. Progress becomes faster than exponential by 2029-30.

## The case for slower progress

The jump from about 8 to about 17 points a year came with reasoning models trained by reinforcement learning, which harvested cheap early gains. Ord estimates reinforcement learning needs about 10,000 times more compute to move a benchmark from 20% to 80%, so that source is nearly used up [capabilities-015, capabilities-016]. Spending growth may peak: a July 2026 estimate saw 2026 as the peak growth year; hyperscalers are borrowing heavily and are projected to have negative free cash flow [capabilities-020, capabilities-022]; customer demand for AI (about 10 times a year) outruns capacity (about 3 times) [capabilities-019], squeezing research compute. The measured AI-driven speed-up rests on redacted internal data and falls to about 1 times on public data [capabilities-008]; Anthropic itself expects doubling research labour to give only 1.15-1.3 times more output [capabilities-006]. Benchmarks are saturating; safety cancellations and legal action have begun [capabilities-023]. Implied: about 204 at end-2028, 228 at end-2031.

## The case for faster progress

The recent pace is itself the product of an acceleration: Epoch's fitted slope doubled when reasoning models arrived [capabilities-002], and the public record has sped up again, from about 15-16 to about 19-21 points a year [capabilities-039]. METR, with inside access, estimates Anthropic's progress since October 2025 at about 1.9 times its earlier rate, attributes most of it to AI doing AI research, rules out faster compute growth as the cause, and saw no bottleneck for the next 12 months [capabilities-007, capabilities-010]. Time-horizon doubling has shortened from 196 to 89 days [capabilities-011]; AI writes over 80% of code at one developer [capabilities-012]. Estimated returns to research effort above 1 imply full automation could raise the pace tenfold within about 1.5 years [capabilities-013, capabilities-031]. Revenue grew 3.5 times in eight months [capabilities-034] and 2027 spending is projected above $1.3 trillion [capabilities-021], compute not yet in any model. Implied: about 255 at end-2028, 450 at end-2031.

## How the forecast was reached

Start: the released record is 174 and the public record has itself sped up, from about 15-16 points a year before October 2025 to about 19-21 since [capabilities-001, capabilities-039]; I add about 5 points for unreleased systems [capabilities-040]. The faster case's strongest evidence is METR's independent finding of about 1.9-times acceleration at Anthropic, mostly due to AI [capabilities-007, capabilities-010]. I give it partial weight: most of its evidence was redacted, it uses Anthropic's own index and internal dates, public data give 0.99-1.4 times [capabilities-008], and Anthropic's own view that doubling research labour yields only 1.15-1.3 times more output points the same way [capabilities-006]. I take current acceleration as about 1.3-1.45 times [capabilities-043]. The two cases disagreed on spending: a July estimate put peak growth in 2026 [capabilities-020]; a November report cites over $1.3 trillion for 2027 [capabilities-021]. These are near-simultaneous estimates with different coverage, so I treat 2027-28 compute as mostly funded and put the financial, power and chip downside mainly in 2029-31 [capabilities-042]. Ord's and You's argument that reinforcement learning scales badly [capabilities-015, capabilities-016] has been wrong on timing so far; I keep it as downside risk, not the centre. What decided the forecast: momentum plus funded compute make less than half the past pace unlikely (about 10%), with pauses and corrections as the main routes there [capabilities-046]; returns-to-research estimates above 1 make sustained acceleration plausible but unproven; unresolved compute bottlenecks [capabilities-017, capabilities-033] and benchmark saturation keep the upper tail wide rather than central [capabilities-047]. Result: a median a little faster than the past pace, with a long upper tail.
